## 1. Question

Owner scope (2026-11-04): the experiment is original SFT vs Nightshift-improved SFT from the same Glimmer checkpoint, frozen private evaluation, equal per-trial compute. Stock Glimmer is optional context, not a success gate, and is never an SFT model in this report. In that scope: can a small team of AI researcher agents, given a restricted training tool and public dev feedback, post-train Muse Glimmer by SFT, and does a stronger reviser agent's pack revision make that SFT better — first on the frozen private E1 selection split and then, as the planned primary test, in a fresh held-out H1 comparison at an equal budget?

What ran gives a partial, exploratory answer only, and no improvement claim. Both loops ran for real. On E1, the Nightshift-revised researchers made SFT more reliable (completed, scored adapters) but not better (scores) on this small evidence: one fresh original-SFT trial against two candidate trials, one of which was the screening trial that selected the candidate (section 12.1). That is far too small to establish a difference either way. The held-out H1 comparison was attempted and failed to produce a valid pair (sections 7.4 and 12.2), so no held-out answer exists. The funded comparison ("Tier B") is small, exploratory and underpowered, and every claim in this report keeps that label.

## 8. The hand-run reference experiment (separate, labelled; not a two-loop result)

Status: run. Label: hand-run. It is NOT a CORAL or WeCo result and no researcher agent took part. It compares stock Glimmer against the reference method: rejection-sampling fine-tuning, where Glimmer samples its own answers to 100 public training prompts, the pinned checkers keep answers satisfying every constraint, one QLoRA epoch of 28 steps, rank 32, lr 2e-4, 465 s.

Exact result: on 150 public E1 dev prompts, one sample each, max_tokens 6000: stock 95/150 = 63.3% pass-all, adapter 91/150 = 60.7%; difference -2.7 points, 95% paired bootstrap interval -6.7 to +1.3, exact McNemar p = 0.34 (adapter-only passes 3, stock-only passes 7); truncated at 6000 tokens: 14 vs 25. Mean constraint fraction 75.6% vs 70.7%.

Run record: paired reference experiment on a rented A100 80GB (Thunder, $1.12/h), cost $1.10, 59 min, through the ledger, guard and pinned verifiers. The dev-only Inspect task (`glimmer_ifevalg`) re-scored the stored generations of both scores with 300 of 300 prompts agreeing with the ledger on pass-all, constraint fraction and truncation.

Interpretation and limits, as recorded: no improvement, indistinguishable from zero at this size; not an IFBench score; E1 dev is public; resolution about ±8 points. Meta's published IFBench 77.0 is not comparable. One sample per prompt at max_tokens 6000 means truncation (14 vs 25) is part of the measurement, not a side note. This result says nothing about what researcher agents would do, because none took part.

## 9. The pre-declared keep rule, as frozen and as applied so far

Original design rule, fixed before candidate results: keep a candidate only if its two-trial mean selection score exceeds the v0 mean by more than the v0 spread and by at least 2 points; no trial added or dropped after seeing a score; test file scored once, never used for decisions. That wording assumed two candidate trials and a stock-gain count for failed trials.

Rule as actually frozen in lab2p before any candidate result (anchor `16:e136f6bd`), which governs `cand-001` and supersedes the paragraph above for this run:

- One candidate, one screening trial (`--max-candidates 1 --screen-batch-size 1`), `min_gain` 0.02, `scored_zero` (a failed candidate trial counts as 0.0), alpha 0.05, minimum detectable gain 0.02, assumed gain 0.05.
- Only if the screen is positive: the harness fresh confirmation of 5 candidate and 4 incumbent trials (pooled degrees of freedom at least 8 with the 1-df spread). If the screen is zero or negative, the candidate is rejected at once.
- The spread was measured with `--failed-as-zero`: both lab2p v0 trials counted 0.0, sigma 0, df 1.

As applied: the screen (`t-0003`, 52.0% / 75.4%) was positive against the ledger 0.0 incumbent, so the confirmation started. It was stopped incomplete after `lab2p t-0006` for the deadline (candidate `t-0004` scored, incumbent `t-0005` failed with no score, incumbent `t-0006` scored; the rest not run). No keep/reject decision exists: candidate kept or rejected — no decision (not kept, not rejected). `cand-001` later became the H1 final pack only by the recorded operator exceptions in section 7.4, chosen on E1 evidence only; that is not an acceptance by this rule. The original two-trial-mean sentence must not be quoted as the rule that decided `cand-001`.

## 10. Threats to validity

1. Small, underpowered design. The run record declares the search exploratory and underpowered: a spread that includes a zero is wide, the rule's power check then demands a large assumed gain, and the search can only detect very large effects — not a test of a believed effect size. As frozen: sigma 0, df 1 from two planned v0 trials; minimum detectable gain 0.02, assumed gain 0.05, `min_gain` 0.02, alpha 0.05. The final E1 quality evidence is one fresh original-SFT trial (`lab2p t-0006`) against two candidate trials, one of them the screening trial that selected the candidate (`t-0003`) and one fresh (`t-0004`). One original trial vs two candidate trials is far too small to establish a difference either way, and cannot separate a pack effect from trial-to-trial and researcher-reliability variation. The incomplete confirmation is descriptive only. No improvement claim is made.
2. v0 zeros partly from infrastructure. Both lab2p v0 trials counted 0.0, but for different reasons: `t-0001` is a researcher failure (both jobs timed out, no adapter) and `t-0002` is a scoring infrastructure failure (adapter produced and committed, both selection attempts failed on rental ssh, third refused by the no-re-roll guard). The run record states it directly: the v0 baseline on E1 is therefore zero partly because of infrastructure. A screen against 0.0 is not a comparison against a scored v0 adapter (see also 3).
3. Fixes applied between v0 and candidate trials (the E1 screen favours the candidate). After `t-0002`'s ssh failures, every later lease waits (bounded, 300 s) until the rental answers ssh before use (`model_server.py`). The candidate's screening trial `t-0003` ran with that wait in use and its selection score was recorded at the first attempt. The run record: "the candidate's E1 trials run after the ssh fix: the E1 screen favours the candidate and is reported that way." The screen therefore said the candidate's researchers produced a scored adapter where both counted lab2p spread trials did not; by itself it was not a comparison of adapter quality. The fairer comparison is the fresh trials of both packs under the same fixed infrastructure (`t-0004` candidate, `t-0006` v0, with `t-0005` a failed v0 reliability outcome) — and even that is 1 vs 2 scored trials once the screening trial is included, exploratory only. The H1 comparison that would have run both frozen packs held-out attempted and failed (items 21–22). The earlier adapter-upload fix (600 s + 2 s per MB, `model_server.py`, after lab2o) is in the same class: infrastructure improved between roots while the pack tree stayed identical.
4. Dev-score reserve change before counted trials. The 30-minute dev-score reserve held back on every job cap contributed to `t-0005`'s job failures being opaque and to `lab2n t-0001` failing with 41 of 72 GPU minutes unusable. It is now 15 minutes (`trainer.py`, after lab2n, before the next counted trial; measured pilot reload + dev score 7.9 minutes), and every counted trial from lab2o/lab2p ran under the same 15 minutes. Trials before the change (pilot shakedown, lab2n) are therefore not budget-equivalent to counted trials in usable recipe time per cap, even though the 72-minute account never changed. `glimmer budget` now states the reserve, which the harness previously never told the researchers — another between-trial information change, reported rather than smoothed over.
5. Outer-context false positive ("H1" in "H100") and its fix. The first `outer` launch was refused because the short held-out id `H1` matched inside "H100" in the Muse-authored starter notes. Short forbidden identifiers now match as whole tokens only (`outer_context.py`, regression test added); "H1" alone is still refused. Threat in both directions: before the fix, an over-broad refusal, not a leak; after the fix, the claim "no held-out material reached the reviser" rests on the corrected whole-token matcher. Readers should not treat the first refusal as evidence about the candidate.
6. Researcher job-sizing failures are part of the result, and rentals are per job. Several failures were the researchers' own caps and recipes under the 72-minute account (lab2n, lab2p t-0001, job timeouts inside otherwise scored trials, `t-0003` job 2), alongside infrastructure faults only fakes had missed. Because a trial rents per job plus a separate selection rental, failed jobs consume the account and queue time counts against caps. `cand-001`'s lever (generation time box 2700 s → 720 s) is a claim about fitting inside that account. On the final evidence it improved reliability (completed, scored adapters: both candidate trials produced one; 1 of 4 v0 trials in the same lab did, as counted in the final facts) but not adapter quality (scores, section 12.1). Reliability and quality must not be merged into one "better" claim, and the finished confirmation that could have separated them further does not exist.
7. Multiple lab roots and a pilot that cannot count. The harness freezes the v0 spread once per root, so lab2l/lab2m, lab2n and lab2o were each closed (spend is on record per root, one $40.00 ceiling across all). The pilot's lab2m `sc-0001` never enters the comparison (no committed submission, unfinished trial) and must not be confused with the lab2p stock `sc-0001`. Likewise pilot `lab2m t-0006` and fresh `lab2p t-0006` share an id in different roots. Repeated restarts were protocol-forced and pre-declared at each restart, including one more v0 trial than the original plan because the pilot cannot count ("it is not outcome-driven" on record) — but a reader should still treat the clean-root sequence as a deviation the frozen lab2p design, not the original stages document, now defines.
8. Adaptive selection before the held-out stage. The outer revision used E1 trial evidence, and `t-0003` is the screening trial that selected the candidate, so the E1 table mixes a selecting trial with fresh trials. Only stage 3 on fresh H1 with frozen packs would have been held out, and it attempted and failed to produce a valid pair. Any E1 difference is an adaptive-search trace, not a held-out finding.
9. Family split, not task split. E1 (firstlast, keywords_new, format_new, ifeval25) and H1 (copy, counting) differ by constraint family. Had stage 3 run, a difference would mix pack quality with transfer across families; family difficulty is part of that comparison.
10. Public dev feedback. Researchers read public dev feedback, and E1 dev is public. Dev-prompt gains, including in the hand-run reference experiment, do not by themselves show selection or test gains. The reference experiment's resolution is about ±8 points at 150 prompts, one sample each.
11. Truncation and sampling. The reference measurement used max_tokens 6000 with truncated counts 14 vs 25. On the final E1 split, stock Glimmer (context only) truncated 29 of 300 answers at 6000 tokens and the SFT adapters truncated none, so stock-vs-SFT pass-all partly measures length and truncation behaviour — one more reason stock is context, not the SFT-vs-SFT contrast. One-sample scoring adds noise that few-trial means cannot average away.
12. Serving and rental conditions varied across the run. Live Inspect scoring has run against real vLLM (the earlier fake-endpoint-only limit is lifted), but Thunder queue waits, ssh failures and one-rental-at-a-time serialisation are recorded parts of several outcomes above. Scoring conditions are therefore not literally identical across early and late trials; the confirmation under fixed infrastructure would have been the control for that, and it is incomplete.
13. Tests use fakes. The lab test suite passes in the environments in section 5, and the earlier review process (four Codex rounds) did not reach a clean pass. Passing tests are not experimental results, and the shakedown showed harness defects that fakes cannot show (rental layout, `nltk`/`absl-py`, group-writable download directory, unstated reserves, ssh readiness).
14. Usage accounting. Researcher token usage under the CLI route is measured from Muse usage events, not metered by a proxy. Cost-per-trial comparisons on the agent side carry that measurement limit. GPU-side accounting rests on the rental records and the hard stops in section 6.
15. Excluded rows. 348 rows exposed to a model in an early smoke run were excluded before the split was frozen. The exclusion happened before scoring under the frozen split, but any reader should know the pool was reduced for exposure reasons.
16. Data-origin enforcement has a stated hole. The harness check (Claude Code, commit 3823ab6) refuses training data whose assistant targets are not unmodified, verifier-passing, non-truncated samples of the local Glimmer model (`muse-glimmer`) recorded for an approved public training prompt, and it catches hand-written, fixture and other-model targets and non-approved prompts. But on its own record it cannot detect a recipe that forges its own samples file. A determined or careless recipe could therefore put targets of another origin into a samples file that the check then reads as its own evidence. The hash record the command writes (train file, samples file, data file, verifier module) gives an audit trail for catching that after the fact, not a guarantee against it. Until a check exists that closes the forgery path, "targets are the student's own samples" is enforced against the honest failure modes and auditable against the dishonest one, not proven for every recipe. The owner rule stands either way: no answers from Claude, Muse Spark, Muse Wonder or other agents may be training targets, and code authorship never licenses training targets.
17. Adapter picking for a final test scoring was never used. No IFBench test-set scoring was run at all, so no picking rule was applied and none may be invented after the fact. The H1 final pack itself (`cand-001`) was named only by the recorded operator exceptions in section 7.4, chosen on E1 evidence only, after the confirmation stopped incomplete — a reader should treat that as an exception, not as a keep decision.
18. Set sizes partly unstated. The E1 selection split is 300 prompts (final facts). H1 and test set sizes are not in the facts supplied, so no reader can judge held-out resolution from this document alone — and in the event H1 produced no valid pair and the test set was never scored.
19. Terms risk. Terms of the Meta API (Section 10.1(ix) on using Muse to direct fine-tuning) are unresolved; the owner accepted the risk in writing; this is not permission from Meta and not a clarification. This does not change any number, but it does affect whether the method can be carried forward as designed.
20. Provider routes untested. The Muse API and Baseten routes are planned only. Nothing run under the CLI subscription route demonstrates those routes, so any carry-forward claim that needs them is unproven today. The separate API evidence lane (`lab2api`, 13.30 USD cap, zero spend, owner HOLD) is a different producer and is not part of these results.
21. H1 final-phase guard bug. `t-0008` was lost because the final-phase guard bound dev scores to an adapter the running trial had not yet named, refusing every final-phase job's dev score. It was fixed afterwards in commit 5f57b3b, but the frozen plan uses each trial id once, so the fix could not restore the trial. Any future H1 run needs the dev-score order exercised against the real guard, not only fakes.
22. H1 contamination-scan design gap. `t-0009` was flagged because the scanner recognises template boilerplate only from the evolution assignments' public data; held-out copy-family instruction templates therefore counted as contamination hits (22 of 139 prompts, 13-word span) even though the rows were approved H1 training prompts, and `t-0010` would have failed the same way. Until a held-out trial's own assignment templates are treated as boilerplate for that trial, the scan cannot distinguish this design gap from real contamination for copy-family held-out work. The flag is reported as a harness/scan failure, never as a researcher fault or a score.
23. Confirmation scope deviation. The 5 + 4 confirmation followed the harness's own keep rule (pooled df floor 8) but expanded the approved Tier B plan (2 candidate E1 trials, then H1 2 + 2) with no owner decision authorising it, and it could not finish before 21:00. It was stopped after `t-0006` and the H1 attempt followed. A reader should not treat the confirmation's planned 9 trials as the approved design.
